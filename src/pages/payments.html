Payments and subscriptions

Gateway checkouts, refunds and vendor-billed subscriptions for seven providers, all written through the ledger and settled only once the vendor confirms them.

Vendors

Paystack, Flutterwave, Stripe, PayPal, OPay, Monnify and Korapay ship. Each is plain Http:: calls on guzzle 8, with no vendor SDK as a dependency. A vendor is offered once every key it needs is set in the environment.

VendorPayments and refundsSubscriptions
Paystack, Flutterwave, Stripe, PayPalYesYes
OPay, Monnify, KorapayYesNo, so they're never offered for a plan

Keys live in .env only, never in the site configuration. An admin screen that can show a secret makes it not a secret. Copy the payments block from .env.example and fill in the vendors you'll use. Leave the rest empty to keep them off. Then check them: 

php artisan payments:health   # one authenticated call to each configured vendor

config/payments.php holds the rest: the checkout expiry (60 minutes), an optional webhook IP allowlist, the queue, the reconcile windows, tracing, and HTTP timeouts.

How a payment is settled

  1. PaymentService::checkout() writes a PROCESSING ledger row and sends the customer to the vendor.
  2. The vendor reports back in up to three ways: it sends the customer to the callback, it posts a webhook, and every ten minutes the reconcile sweep asks it directly.
  3. All three end in PaymentService::verify(), which asks the vendor, locks the row, and settles it once. A second answer does nothing.

Nothing the browser carries back is believed. The callback URL contains only the kit's own reference, and the status always comes from the vendor. Never settle a PAYMENT_GATEWAY row any other way.

  • Short or wrong-currency money is held. The row stays PROCESSING with failure_reason set, and admins are notified. Money landing on a row that's already closed becomes CONFIRMED_BUT_NOT_LOGGED. A person settles both.
  • Webhooks arrive at POST /webhooks/payments/{vendor}. The signature is checked before the body is read, the call is stored in payment_webhooks (duplicates are dropped by unique(vendor, event_key)), and the work happens on the queue. Turning signature checks off is honoured in local and testing only.
  • The callback, GET /payments/{vendor}/callback/{reference}, is outside the auth group, so a session that expires during checkout doesn't cost the customer their confirmation. It's sent with each checkout, so you don't register it anywhere.
  • Tracing (PAYMENTS_TRACING, off by default) stores one redacted row per vendor call in payment_traces, kept for 30 days.

Taking a payment for something

Members get Add funds on their transactions page, which is a deposit. To charge for something of your own, pass its group, the DIRECT type, the NONE wallet, and the model being paid for: 

$result = app(PaymentService::class)->checkout(
    $user,
    PaymentVendorEnum::from($this->vendor),
    $order->total,
    "Order {$order->number}",
    TransactionGroupEnum::PURCHASE,
    TransactionTypeEnum::DIRECT,
    TransactionWalletEnum::NONE,
    against: $order,
);

$this->respondError($result, \is_string($result));

return $this->redirect($result->gateway->gateway_url);

Fulfil the order in a listener, so the kit never needs to know what an order is:

// app/Listeners/FulfilOrders.php, discovered automatically
public function handle(PaymentSucceeded $event): void
{
    if ($event->transaction->transactionable instanceof Order) {
        app(OrderService::class)->markPaid($event->transaction->transactionable);
    }
}

The events are PaymentInitialized, PaymentSucceeded, PaymentFailed, PaymentNeedsReview, PaymentWebhookReceived, RefundInitiated, RefundProcessed, RefundFailed, SubscriptionStarted, SubscriptionRenewed, SubscriptionPaymentFailed, SubscriptionCancelled and SubscriptionEnded.

Processing fees

Each vendor can carry a processing fee: a percentage of the price, plus a flat amount, up to an optional cap (0 means no cap). Set it from the Processing fee column on Payments → Gateways. It's stored in the site configuration under payments.fees. Every fee is zero until someone sets one, because passing the card cost on to customers is the owner's decision.

  • checkout() adds the fee to a purchase. The row's amount is the total the vendor is asked for, so verify() checks against the same figure. The fee is also kept as a TransactionChargeEnum::FEE charge row, so a receipt can show it.
  • A wallet deposit, including Add funds, never carries a fee, because its amount is what lands in the wallet. Manual vendors never carry one either.
  • A change to a fee is logged with the old and new terms, and applies from the next checkout. A payment that's already open keeps its fee.

Tell the customer before they leave for the vendor. Call app(PaymentFeeService::class)->feeFor($vendor, $price) in the page's #[Computed] and show it next to the total. Don't work the fee out anywhere else.

Refunds

A refund is its own REFUND row pointing at the payment. The payment isn't edited. It stays CONFIRMED, because it happened. Admins refund from the Transactions screen, which also has "check with provider".

  • Partial or full. What's left to refund is the payment minus every refund that's CONFIRMED or PROCESSING. A failed refund frees its amount again.
  • Locked. The check and the new row are written under a lock on the payment, so two admins can't refund more than came in between them. A deposit the member has partly spent can't be refunded past their balance.
  • Through the vendor or by hand. A gateway payment is refunded through the vendor and stays PROCESSING until the vendor confirms it. An approved bank transfer is paid back by hand and recorded as CONFIRMED straight away.
  • A refund made from the vendor's own dashboard arrives as a webhook with nothing behind it, so it raises PaymentNeedsReview.

Subscriptions

The vendor does the billing: it holds the card, runs the schedule, and retries failed charges. Admins manage plans on Subscriptions (gate subscriptions), and members choose one on their own Subscriptions page.

PieceHow it works
Plansubscription_plans. It can be free, recommended, carry trial days, or include another plan ("Everything in Basic, plus")
Pricesubscription_plan_prices, one row per interval. The card works out what a longer interval saves. Each price is synced to a vendor when its first subscriber arrives
Featuressubscription_plan_features, one row per SubscriptionFeatureEnum case, with an optional limit (null means unlimited)
Free planAt most one. It's the fallback when nothing else gives access, with no row per account
TrialRun by the kit, not the vendor, with no card. One per account, ended as soon as the member pays
RenewalArrives by webhook and is checked with the vendor. Each charge is recorded once
Failed renewalThe subscription becomes PAST_DUE. Whether access continues is up to your project
CancelBilling stops. Access lasts until current_period_ends_at, then the hourly sync marks it EXPIRED

Gating a paid feature

Ask about a feature, never about a plan name:

app(SubscriptionService::class)->allows($user, SubscriptionFeatureEnum::API_ACCESS);
app(SubscriptionService::class)->limitFor($user, SubscriptionFeatureEnum::PROJECTS);
// limitFor(): null is unlimited, 0 is not granted

Both count a cancelled subscription with time left and a running trial, and fall back to the free plan.

The four shipped SubscriptionFeatureEnum cases are examples. Replace them with your own before creating plans. A case can't be removed while plan rows still use it.

No coupons ship. A yearly price below twelve monthly ones is the built-in discount.

Going live

  • Register https://your-site/webhooks/payments/{vendor} as the webhook URL in each vendor's dashboard. webhooks/* is already exempt from CSRF.
  • Run a queue worker. Webhooks are processed on the queue, and the reconcile sweep is only a safety net.
  • Grant the payments and subscriptions gates to the roles that should see those screens.

Adding a vendor

  1. A case in PaymentVendorEnum, with its label() and gateway() arms.
  2. A block under payments.vendors in config/payments.php, and the keys in .env.example.
  3. app/Services/Payment/Gateways/{Vendor}GatewayService.php, extending BasePaymentGateway. It implements initialize(), verify(), verifyWebhook(), parseWebhook() and ping().
  4. Tests: a signature test using the vendor's real scheme, and a checkout-and-verify test with Http::fake().

The full contract is in .agents/my-skills/payments.md in the kit.
