Legal pages and consent

Terms, privacy and cookie pages that your install serves to its own users, with consent recorded against the exact version accepted.

This page is about the legal pages your application serves. For the legal terms of Ezeh Start itself, see License, Trademark policy and Disclaimer.

The pages

/terms, /privacy and /cookies are written in the rich-text editor from Admin → Policies, sanitised on save by BlogService::sanitize(), and served as plain, crawlable pages with a contents sidebar. Each one is a case in PolicyTypeEnum, and adding a case gives you a new public page automatically. The public footer links them straight from the enum.

Every Heading 2 starts a section with its own anchor. Legal pages get linked from contracts and emails nobody can go back and edit, so pin an anchor at the end of the heading to keep it working after the heading is reworded: 5. Refunds {#refunds}. The preview on the admin screen shows where each anchor lands.

App\Models\Policy is a legal document, not a Laravel authorization policy. The kit uses no authorization policies at all.

Versioning

  • Published text is never edited. To change it, draft a new version and publish that.
  • Older versions stay readable at ?version=, because consent records point at them.

Telling members

The publish dialog has an Email all members about this update box. It's unticked by default and shows how many accounts it would reach. When it's ticked, every active member gets a short notice with the policy's intro and a link to the version now in force.

  • Admins, newsletter-only addresses, and suspended or deleting accounts aren't emailed.
  • It's a notice about the service, not marketing, so an account's email switch isn't checked.
  • The emails are queued after the response, one each, in chunks of 200, so they need a queue worker. One that fails to queue is logged and doesn't stop the rest.
  • The activity log records how many members were emailed.

Consent

  • Terms and privacy are marked as requiring consent, so registration asks for it.
  • user_consents records the version accepted, with the timestamp, IP address and user agent.
  • When a replacement is published, every account has to accept it again.

Before you go live

The DemoSeeder policies only show the shape of a policy. They aren't legal advice and aren't written for your jurisdiction or business. Have your own terms and privacy policy written, then publish them as a new version.
